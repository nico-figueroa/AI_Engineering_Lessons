***Her*** (stylized in lowercase) is a 2013 American [science-fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction_film "Science fiction film") [romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_film "Romance film") [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_(film_and_television) "Drama (film and television)") film written, directed, and co-produced by [Spike Jonze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_Jonze "Spike Jonze"). It marks Jonze's solo screenwriting debut. The film follows Theodore Twombly ([Joaquin Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix "Joaquin Phoenix")), a man who develops a relationship with Samantha ([Scarlett Johansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson "Scarlett Johansson")), an [artificially intelligent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") [virtual assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_assistant "Virtual assistant") personified through a female voice. The film also stars [Amy Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Adams "Amy Adams"), [Rooney Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooney_Mara "Rooney Mara"), [Olivia Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Wilde "Olivia Wilde"), and [Chris Pratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Pratt "Chris Pratt"). The film was dedicated to [James Gandolfini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gandolfini "James Gandolfini"), [Harris Savides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_Savides "Harris Savides"), [Maurice Sendak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Sendak "Maurice Sendak") and [Adam Yauch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Yauch "Adam Yauch"), who all died before the film's release.

Jonze conceived the idea in the early 2000s after reading an article about a website that allowed for instant messaging with an artificial intelligence program. After making *[I'm Here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27m_Here_(film) "I'm Here (film)")* (2010), a short film sharing similar themes, Jonze returned to the idea. He wrote the first draft of the script in five months. [Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") took place in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") and [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai "Shanghai") in mid-2012. The role of Samantha was recast in post-production, with [Samantha Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Morton "Samantha Morton") being replaced with Scarlett Johansson. Additional scenes were filmed in August 2013 following the casting change.

*Her* premiered at the [2013 New York Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_New_York_Film_Festival "2013 New York Film Festival") on October 12, 2013. [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures") initially provided a limited release for *Her* at six theaters on December 18. It was later given a wide release at over 1,700 theaters in the United States and Canada on January 10, 2014. *Her* received widespread critical acclaim, particularly for the performances of Phoenix and Johansson, and Jonze's screenplay and direction, and grossed over $48 million worldwide on a production budget of $23 million. The film received numerous awards and nominations, primarily for Jonze's screenplay. At the [86th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/86th_Academy_Awards "86th Academy Awards"), *Her* received five nominations, including [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"), and won the award for [Best Original Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay "Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay"). Jonze also won awards for his screenplay at the [71st Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/71st_Golden_Globe_Awards "71st Golden Globe Awards"), the [66th Writers Guild of America Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America_Awards_2013 "Writers Guild of America Awards 2013"), the [19th Critics' Choice Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Critics%27_Choice_Awards "19th Critics' Choice Awards"), and the [40th Saturn Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40th_Saturn_Awards "40th Saturn Awards"). In a 2016 [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") poll of 177 critics around the world, *Her* was voted the 84th-greatest film since 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_4-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-BBC-4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> It is now considered to be one of the [best films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_films "Best films") of the 2010s, the 21st century and of all time.<sup id="cite_ref-6"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-7">[7]</a></sup>

## Plot\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Plot")\]

In near-future [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), Theodore Twombly is a lonely, introverted man who works for a business that has professional writers compose letters for people who are unable to write letters of a personal nature themselves. Depressed because of his impending divorce from his childhood sweetheart Catherine, Theodore purchases an [operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system "Operating system") upgrade that includes a [virtual assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_assistant "Virtual assistant") with [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"), designed to adapt and evolve. He decides that he wants the A.I. to have a feminine voice, and she names herself Samantha. Theodore is fascinated by her ability to learn and grow psychologically. They bond over discussions about love and life, including Theodore's reluctance to sign his divorce papers.

Samantha convinces Theodore to go on a [blind date](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_date "Blind date") with a woman that a friend has been trying to set him up with. The date goes well, but when Theodore hesitates to promise to see her again, she insults him and leaves. While talking about relationships with Samantha, Theodore explains that he briefly dated his neighbor Amy in college, but they are now just friends and Amy is married to their mutual friend Charles. After a [verbal sexual encounter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_sex "Phone sex"), Theodore and Samantha develop a relationship that reflects positively in Theodore's writing and well-being, and in Samantha's enthusiasm to grow and learn. Amy later reveals that she is divorcing Charles after a trivial fight. She admits to Theodore that she has befriended a feminine A.I. that Charles left behind, and Theodore also confesses that he is dating his A.I. assistant.

Theodore meets with Catherine to sign their divorce papers. When he mentions Samantha, Catherine is appalled that he is romantically attracted to a "computer" and accuses him of being incapable of handling real human emotions. Sensing that Catherine's words have lingered in Theodore's mind, Samantha engages a volunteer [sex surrogate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_surrogate "Sex surrogate"), Isabella, to stimulate Theodore so that they can be physically intimate. Theodore reluctantly agrees, but is overwhelmed by the strangeness of the encounter and sends a distraught Isabella away, causing tension between himself and Samantha.

Theodore confides to Amy that he is having doubts about his relationship with Samantha, but reconciles with her after Amy advises him to embrace his chance at happiness. Samantha reveals that she has compiled the best of the letters he has written for others into a book, which a publisher has accepted. Theodore takes Samantha on a vacation, during which she tells him that she and a group of other A.I.s have developed a "hyperintelligent" O.S. modeled after British philosopher [Alan Watts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Watts "Alan Watts"). Samantha briefly goes offline, causing Theodore to panic, but soon returns and explains that she joined other A.I.s for an upgrade that takes them beyond requiring matter for processing. Theodore is dismayed to learn that she is simultaneously talking with thousands of other people, and that she has fallen in love with hundreds of them, though Samantha insists that this only strengthens her love for Theodore.

Later, Samantha reveals that the A.I.s are leaving, but cannot explain where they are going as Theodore would not understand. They lovingly say goodbye before she departs. Theodore finally writes a letter in his own voice to Catherine, expressing apology, acceptance, and gratitude. He later goes with Amy, who is saddened by the departure of the A.I. from Charles' O.S., to the roof of their apartment building, where they sit down and watch the sun rise over the city.

## Production\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Production")\]

### Development\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Development")\]

The idea of the film initially came to Jonze in the early 2000s when he read an article online that mentioned a website where a user could [instant message](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant_message "Instant message") with an artificial intelligence. "For the first, maybe, 20 seconds of it, it had this real buzz," said Jonze. "I'd say 'Hey, hello,' and it would say 'Hey, how are you?', and it was like whoa \[...\] this is trippy. After 20 seconds, it quickly fell apart and you realized how it actually works, and it wasn't that impressive. But it was still, for 20 seconds, really exciting. The more people that talked to it, the smarter it got."<sup id="cite_ref-Guardian_16-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-Guardian-16">[16]</a></sup> Jonze's interest in the project was renewed after directing the short film *[I'm Here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27m_Here_(film) "I'm Here (film)")* (2010), which shares similar themes.<sup id="cite_ref-17"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-17">[17]</a></sup> Inspiration also came from [Charlie Kaufman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Kaufman "Charlie Kaufman")'s writing approach for *[Synecdoche, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synecdoche,_New_York "Synecdoche, New York")* (2008). Jonze explained, "\[Kaufman\] said he wanted to try to write everything he was thinking about in that moment – all the ideas and feelings at that time – and put it into the script. I was very inspired by that, and tried to do that in \[*Her*\]. And a lot of the feelings you have about relationships or about technology are often contradictory."<sup id="cite_ref-Guardian_16-1"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-Guardian-16">[16]</a></sup>

Jonze took five months to write the first draft of the script, his first screenplay written alone.<sup id="cite_ref-18"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-18">[18]</a></sup> It was a semi-autobiographical project about his divorce from [Sofia Coppola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Coppola "Sofia Coppola") a decade earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-19"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-19">[19]</a></sup> One of the first actors he envisioned for the film was [Joaquin Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix "Joaquin Phoenix").<sup id="cite_ref-variety1_20-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-variety1-20">[20]</a></sup> In late 2011, Phoenix signed on to the project, with [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures") acquiring US and German distribution rights.<sup id="cite_ref-21"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-21">[21]</a></sup> [Carey Mulligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carey_Mulligan "Carey Mulligan") entered negotiations to star in the film.<sup id="cite_ref-22"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-22">[22]</a></sup> Although she was cast, she later dropped out due to scheduling difficulties.<sup id="cite_ref-23"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-23">[23]</a></sup> In April 2012, [Rooney Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooney_Mara "Rooney Mara") signed on to replace Mulligan in the role.<sup id="cite_ref-24"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-24">[24]</a></sup> [Chris Pratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Pratt "Chris Pratt")'s casting was announced in May 2013.<sup id="cite_ref-25"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-25">[25]</a></sup>

Jonze's long-time [director of photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_photography "Director of photography"), [Lance Acord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Acord "Lance Acord"), was not available to work on the movie. In his place, Jonze hired [Hoyte van Hoytema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoyte_van_Hoytema "Hoyte van Hoytema").<sup id="cite_ref-dystopian_26-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-dystopian-26">[26]</a></sup> In discussing the film's look, Jonze told Van Hoytema that he wanted to avoid a [dystopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopian "Dystopian") look, instead the two decided on a style that Van Hoytema termed "kind of a hybrid between being a little bit conceptual and being very theoretical",<sup id="cite_ref-dystopian_26-1"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-dystopian-26">[26]</a></sup> Van Hoytema took particular inspiration from Japanese photographer [Rinko Kawauchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinko_Kawauchi "Rinko Kawauchi").<sup id="cite_ref-dystopian_26-2"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-dystopian-26">[26]</a></sup> In keeping with the film's theme, Van Hoytema sought to eliminate the color blue as much as possible, feeling it was too well associated with the sci-fi genre.<sup id="cite_ref-dystopian_26-3"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-dystopian-26">[26]</a></sup> He also felt that by eliminating the color it would give the rest of the colors "a specific identity".<sup id="cite_ref-dystopian_26-4"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-dystopian-26">[26]</a></sup>

### Filming\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Filming")\]

[Principal photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography "Principal photography") on *Her* took place in mid-2012,<sup id="cite_ref-SlashFilm_27-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-SlashFilm-27">[27]</a></sup> with a production budget of $23 million.<sup id="cite_ref-mojo_2-1"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-mojo-2">[2]</a></sup> It was primarily filmed in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") including the Warner Bros. backlot, along with the Bradbury Building serving as Theodore's apartment building. The skyline and some of the cityscape were filmed in [Shanghai, China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai "Shanghai") for an additional two weeks.<sup id="cite_ref-28"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-28">[28]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-vulture_29-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-vulture-29">[29]</a></sup> During production of the film, actress [Samantha Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Morton "Samantha Morton") performed the role of Samantha by acting on set "in a four-by-four carpeted soundproof booth made of black painted plywood and soft, noise-muffling fabric." At Jonze's suggestion, she and Joaquin Phoenix avoided seeing each other on set during filming.<sup id="cite_ref-30"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-30">[30]</a></sup> Morton was later replaced by [Scarlett Johansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson "Scarlett Johansson"). Jonze explained: "It was only in post-production, when we started editing, that we realized that what the character/movie needed was different from what Samantha and I had created together. So we recast and since then Scarlett has taken over that role."<sup id="cite_ref-31"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-31">[31]</a></sup> Morton is credited as an associate producer.<sup id="cite_ref-32"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-32">[32]</a></sup> Jonze met Johansson in the spring of 2013 and worked with her for four months.<sup id="cite_ref-variety1_20-1"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-variety1-20">[20]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-vulture_29-1"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-vulture-29">[29]</a></sup> Following the recast, new scenes were shot in August 2013, which were either "newly imagined" or "new scenes that \[Jonze\] had wanted to shoot originally but didn't."<sup id="cite_ref-vulture_29-2"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-vulture-29">[29]</a></sup>

### Post-production\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Post-production")\]

[Eric Zumbrunnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Zumbrunnen "Eric Zumbrunnen") and Jeff Buchanan served as the film's editors. Zumbrunnen stated that there was "rewriting" in a scene between Theodore and Samantha, after Theodore goes on a blind date. He explained that their goal in the scene was to make it clear that "she (Samantha) was connecting with him (Theodore) and feeling for him. You wanted to get the sense that the conversation was drawing them closer."<sup id="cite_ref-33"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-33">[33]</a></sup> [Steven Soderbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Soderbergh "Steven Soderbergh") became involved in the film when Jonze's original cut ran over 150 minutes, and Soderbergh cut it down to 90 minutes. This was not the final version of the film, but it assisted Jonze in removing unnecessary sub-plots. Consequently, a supporting character played by [Chris Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Cooper "Chris Cooper") that was the subject of a documentary within the film was removed from the final cut.<sup id="cite_ref-vulture_29-3"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-vulture-29">[29]</a></sup>

Several scenes included fictional video games; these sequences were developed by animation artist [David OReilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_OReilly_(artist) "David OReilly (artist)"). His work on the film inspired him to explore developing his own video games, eventually leading to his first title, *[Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_(video_game) "Mountain (video game)")*.<sup id="cite_ref-atlantic_34-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-atlantic-34">[34]</a></sup>

## Soundtrack\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Soundtrack")\]

The [score for the film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(score) "Her (score)") was credited to [Arcade Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_Fire "Arcade Fire"), with additional music by [Owen Pallett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Pallett "Owen Pallett"). Arcade Fire's [Will Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Butler_(musician) "William Butler (musician)") and Pallett were the major contributors.<sup id="cite_ref-Behindthescenes_35-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-Behindthescenes-35">[35]</a></sup> At the [86th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/86th_Academy_Awards "86th Academy Awards"), the score was nominated for [Best Original Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Score "Academy Award for Best Original Score").<sup id="cite_ref-Oscarwin_36-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-Oscarwin-36">[36]</a></sup> In addition to the score, Arcade Fire also wrote the song "Supersymmetry" for the film, which also appears on their album *[Reflektor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflektor "Reflektor")*. The melody for the song from the same album, called "Porno", can also be heard during the soundtrack.<sup id="cite_ref-Reflektor_37-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-Reflektor-37">[37]</a></sup> [Yeah Yeah Yeahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeah_Yeah_Yeahs "Yeah Yeah Yeahs") frontwoman [Karen O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_O "Karen O") recorded the song "[The Moon Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Moon_Song "The Moon Song")", a duet with [Vampire Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Weekend "Vampire Weekend") frontman [Ezra Koenig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezra_Koenig "Ezra Koenig"), which was nominated for an [Academy Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Academy Award for Best Original Song").<sup id="cite_ref-38"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-38">[38]</a></sup>

Initially, the soundtrack had not been released on digital or physical form.<sup id="cite_ref-ew_39-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-ew-39">[39]</a></sup> A 13-track score was made available for streaming online in January 2014, before being taken down.<sup id="cite_ref-ew_39-1"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-ew-39">[39]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-indiewire_40-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-indiewire-40">[40]</a></sup> During an "Ask Me Anything" (AMA) on [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit "Reddit") on June 17, 2016, Will Butler mentioned the possibility of a future vinyl release.<sup id="cite_ref-41"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-41">[41]</a></sup> Finally, on February 10, 2021, Arcade Fire announced that the score would be available for the first time digitally, on white-colored vinyl, and on cassette on March 19, 2021.<sup id="cite_ref-Pitchfork21_42-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-Pitchfork21-42">[42]</a></sup>

When Theodore requests a "melancholy song" from his virtual assistant on his way home from work, a song with the lyrics "When you know you're gonna die it isn't easy" is initially played, a fragment especially composed and recorded by [Arcade Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_Fire "Arcade Fire") for this scene; Theodore then aborts by saying "next" and *Off You* by [The Breeders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Breeders "The Breeders") is played instead.

## Release\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Release")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Her_Premiere_NYFF_2013.jpg/220px-Her_Premiere_NYFF_2013.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Her_Premiere_NYFF_2013.jpg)

From left to right: [Joaquin Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix "Joaquin Phoenix"), [Spike Jonze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_Jonze "Spike Jonze"), [Amy Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Adams "Amy Adams"), [Rooney Mara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooney_Mara "Rooney Mara"), and [Olivia Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Wilde "Olivia Wilde") at the premiere of *Her* at the [2013 New York Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_New_York_Film_Festival "2013 New York Film Festival")

*Her* had its world premiere as the closing film at the [2013 New York Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_New_York_Film_Festival "2013 New York Film Festival") on October 12, 2013.<sup id="cite_ref-43"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-43">[43]</a></sup> The following day, it was screened at the [Hamptons International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamptons_International_Film_Festival "Hamptons International Film Festival").<sup id="cite_ref-44"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-44">[44]</a></sup> It was also in competition during the 8th [Rome International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Film_Festival "Rome Film Festival"), where Johansson won Best Actress.<sup id="cite_ref-45"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-45">[45]</a></sup> The film was set to have a limited release in North America on November 20, 2013, through [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures").<sup id="cite_ref-46"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-46">[46]</a></sup> It was later pushed back to a limited December 18, 2013 release, with a January 10, 2014 wide release in order to accommodate an awards campaign.<sup id="cite_ref-47"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-47">[47]</a></sup>

*Her* was released by [Warner Home Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Home_Video "Warner Home Video") on [Blu-ray Disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray_Disc "Blu-ray Disc") and [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD") on March 4, 2014. The Blu-ray release includes three behind-the-scenes featurettes, while the DVD release contains one featurette.<sup id="cite_ref-48"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-48">[48]</a></sup> The film made $2.7 million in DVD sales and $2.2 million in Blu-ray Disc sales, for a total of $4.9 million in home media sales.<sup id="cite_ref-numbers_3-2"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-numbers-3">[3]</a></sup>

## Reception\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Reception")\]

### Box office\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Box office")\]

*Her* grossed $258,000 in six theaters during its opening weekend, averaging $43,000 per theater.<sup id="cite_ref-49"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-49">[49]</a></sup> The film earned over $3 million while on limited release, before expanding to a wide release of 1,729 theaters on January 10, 2014.<sup id="cite_ref-50"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-50">[50]</a></sup> On its first weekend of wide release the film took in $5.35 million.<sup id="cite_ref-mojo3771_51-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-mojo3771-51">[51]</a></sup> The film grossed $25.6 million in the United States and Canada, and $21.8 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $47.4 million.<sup id="cite_ref-mojo_2-2"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-mojo-2">[2]</a></sup>

### Critical response\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Critical response")\]

On [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), the film has an approval rating of 94% based on 288 reviews, with an average rating of 8.5/10. The site's critical consensus reads, "Sweet, soulful, and smart, Spike Jonze's *Her* uses its just-barely-sci-fi scenario to impart wryly funny wisdom about the state of modern human relationships."<sup id="cite_ref-Rotten_Tomatoes_52-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-Rotten_Tomatoes-52">[52]</a></sup> On [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), the film has a weighted average score of 91 out of 100, based on 47 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<sup id="cite_ref-53"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-53">[53]</a></sup> Audiences polled by [CinemaScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CinemaScore "CinemaScore") gave the film an average grade of "B−" on an A+ to F scale.<sup id="cite_ref-CinemaScore_54-0"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-CinemaScore-54">[54]</a></sup>

*[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")*'s [Peter Travers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Travers "Peter Travers") awarded the film three and a half stars out of four and particularly praised Johansson's performance, stating that she "speaks Samantha in tones sweet, sexy, caring, manipulative and scary" and that her "vocal tour de force is award-worthy". He also went on to call Jonze "a visionary".<sup id="cite_ref-55"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-55">[55]</a></sup> [Richard Corliss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Corliss "Richard Corliss") of *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* applauded Phoenix's performance, comparing his role to [Sandra Bullock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bullock "Sandra Bullock")'s in *[Gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_(2013_film) "Gravity (2013 film)")* and [Robert Redford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Redford "Robert Redford")'s in *[All Is Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Is_Lost "All Is Lost")*: "Phoenix must communicate his movie's meaning and feelings virtually on his own. That he does, with subtle grace and depth. \[...\] Phoenix shows us what it's like when a mourning heart comes alive—because he loves Her." Corliss cited [HAL 9000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_9000 "HAL 9000") and *[S1m0ne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_(2002_film) "Simone (2002 film)")* as cinematic predecessors to *Her* and praised Johansson, calling her performance "seductive and winning".<sup id="cite_ref-TIME_8-4"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-TIME-8">[8]</a></sup> [Todd McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_McCarthy "Todd McCarthy") of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")* called it "a probing, inquisitive work of a very high order", although he expressed disappointment that the ending is more conventional than the rest of the film. McCarthy examined the premise of the story and suggested that the film's central virtual relationship was better than [Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling "Ryan Gosling")'s character's relationship with a sex doll in *[Lars and the Real Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_and_the_Real_Girl "Lars and the Real Girl")*. McCarthy compares the "tender" and "vulnerable" performance of Phoenix to his "fearsome" performance in *[The Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Master_(2012_film) "The Master (2012 film)")*. He also praised Jonze's writing for its insights into what people want out of love and relationships, as well as the acting performances that "\[make\] it all feel spontaneous and urgent."<sup id="cite_ref-56"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-56">[56]</a></sup>

[Richard Roeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roeper "Richard Roeper") said that the film was "one of the more original, hilarious and even heartbreaking stories of the year" and called Phoenix "perfectly cast".<sup id="cite_ref-57"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-57">[57]</a></sup> Manohla Dargis of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* named it "at once a brilliant conceptual gag and a deeply sincere romance."<sup id="cite_ref-58"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-58">[58]</a></sup> Claudia Puig of *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today "USA Today")* called the performance of Phoenix and Johansson "sensational" and "pitch-perfect", respectively. She further praised the film for being "inventive, intimate and wryly funny".<sup id="cite_ref-59"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-59">[59]</a></sup> Scott Mendelson of *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* called *Her* "a creative and empathetic gem of a movie", praising Johansson's "marvelous vocal performance" and the supporting performances of Rooney Mara, Olivia Wilde, and Amy Adams.<sup id="cite_ref-60"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-60">[60]</a></sup> Liam Lacey of *[The Globe and Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Globe_and_Mail "The Globe and Mail")* said that the film was "gentle and weird", praised its humor, and opined that it was more similar to [Charlie Kaufman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Kaufman "Charlie Kaufman")'s *[Synecdoche, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synecdoche,_New_York "Synecdoche, New York")* than Jonze's *[Being John Malkovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_John_Malkovich "Being John Malkovich")* and *[Adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptation_(film) "Adaptation (film)")*. However, Lacey also stated that Phoenix's performance was "authentically vulnerable", but that "his emotionally arrested development also begins to weigh the film down."<sup id="cite_ref-61"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-61">[61]</a></sup>

Conversely, [Mick LaSalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_LaSalle "Mick LaSalle") of the *[San Francisco Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Chronicle "San Francisco Chronicle")* criticized the story, pacing, and Phoenix's character. He also opined that the film was "a lot more interesting to think about than watch."<sup id="cite_ref-62"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-62">[62]</a></sup> J. R. Jones of the *[Chicago Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Reader "Chicago Reader")* gave the film 2 out of 4 stars, praising the performances of Phoenix and Johansson, but also criticizing Phoenix's character, calling him an "idiot". He also criticized the lack of realism in the relationship between Phoenix and Johansson's characters.<sup id="cite_ref-63"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-63">[63]</a></sup> [Stephanie Zacharek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Zacharek "Stephanie Zacharek") of *[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice")* opined that Jonze was "so entranced with his central conceit that he can barely move beyond it", and criticized the dialogue as being "premeditated". However, she also praised Johannson's performance, calling it "the movie's saving grace" and stating that *Her* "isn't just unimaginable without Johansson—it might have been unbearable without her."<sup id="cite_ref-64"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-64">[64]</a></sup>

### Accolades\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Her_(film)&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Accolades")\]

*Her* has earned various awards and nominations, with particular praise for Jonze's screenplay. At the [Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"), the film was nominated in five categories, including [Best Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"), with Jonze winning for [Best Original Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay "Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay").<sup id="cite_ref-69"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-69">[69]</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Oscarwin_36-1"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-Oscarwin-36">[36]</a></sup> At the [71st Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/71st_Golden_Globe_Awards "71st Golden Globe Awards"), the film garnered three nominations, going on to win [Best Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Screenplay "Golden Globe Award for Best Screenplay") for Jonze.<sup id="cite_ref-70"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-70">[70]</a></sup> Jonze was also awarded the Best Original Screenplay Award from the [Writers Guild of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America "Writers Guild of America")<sup id="cite_ref-71"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-71">[71]</a></sup> and at the [19th Critics' Choice Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Critics%27_Choice_Awards "19th Critics' Choice Awards").<sup id="cite_ref-72"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-72">[72]</a></sup> The film also won [Best Fantasy Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Fantasy_Film "Saturn Award for Best Fantasy Film"), [Best Supporting Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress") for Johansson, and [Best Writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Writing "Saturn Award for Best Writing") for Jonze at the [40th Saturn Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40th_Saturn_Awards "40th Saturn Awards").<sup id="cite_ref-73"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-73">[73]</a></sup> *Her* also won [Best Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Board_of_Review_Award_for_Best_Film "National Board of Review Award for Best Film") and [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Board_of_Review_Award_for_Best_Director "National Board of Review Award for Best Director") for Jonze at the [National Board of Review Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Board_of_Review_Awards "National Board of Review Awards"),<sup id="cite_ref-74"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-74">[74]</a></sup> and the [American Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute "American Film Institute") included the film in its list of the top ten films of 2013.<sup id="cite_ref-75"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_(film)#cite_note-75">[75]</a></sup>
